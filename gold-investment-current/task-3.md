# Task 3: Gold Returns vs. Equities, Bonds, and Inflation (Past 5 Years)

## Overview of 5-Year Performance (2020–2025)

Over the past five years (2020–2025), gold has emerged as the clear outperformer among traditional asset classes, delivering annualized returns of **18.4%** compared to global equities at **12.5%**, corporate bonds at just **1.3%**, and sovereign bonds at **-1.1%** annually. This period, beginning with the COVID-19 pandemic and extending through persistent geopolitical tensions and inflationary pressures, created ideal conditions for gold to shine as both a safe-haven asset and a hedge against economic uncertainty. Gold's cumulative performance demonstrates its value as a portfolio diversifier during volatile market conditions, significantly outpacing equities and substantially outperforming both bond categories that suffered from rising interest rates and inflation.

## Drivers of Gold's Outperformance

Gold's exceptional 5-year returns were driven by several interconnected factors. **Economic uncertainty** stemming from the pandemic, coupled with unprecedented fiscal stimulus and monetary easing, bolstered demand for safe-haven assets. **Geopolitical tensions**, particularly the Russia-Ukraine conflict in 2022, prompted investors and central banks to seek alternative reserve assets. **Inflationary pressures** that emerged in 2021–2023 highlighted gold's role as an inflation hedge, with the asset maintaining purchasing power as consumer prices surged globally. **Central bank demand** remained robust, as major central banks increased gold reserves for currency diversification. Additionally, **weakness in the U.S. dollar** in 2025—declining roughly 10%—inversely supported gold prices, making the metal more attractive to international investors.

## Comparative Risk and Volatility

While gold delivered higher absolute returns than equities and bonds over this period, the path was more volatile. Gold exhibited **volatility of 15.4%**, comparable to global equities at **16.8%** but significantly higher than sovereign bonds (**5.8%**) and corporate bonds (**6.3%**). Notably, gold's volatility profile remained consistent despite outperforming, demonstrating that higher returns did not require proportionally higher risk. For context, global equities also faced **16.8% volatility** while delivering lower returns, illustrating that gold's risk-adjusted performance was favorable. The poor performance of bonds—particularly sovereign bonds declining at **-1.1% annually**—reflects the impact of interest rate hikes on fixed-income valuations, making them particularly unsuitable for inflation protection despite their traditional low-volatility classification.

## Long-Term Context: Five-Year Performance Within Broader Trends

The past five years represent a sharp reversal from the preceding decade (2010–2025), when global equities significantly outperformed gold. From 2010–2025, equities delivered **10.5% annualized returns** versus gold's **8.6%**, despite both experiencing considerable volatility. However, the 2020–2025 window shows that gold's decade-long underperformance has been fully reversed and then some. Over a 35-year horizon (1990–2025), global equities maintain a slight edge with **8.1% annualized returns** compared to gold's **6.7%**, but this masks the critical insight that gold's performance is highly cyclical and sensitive to inflation, monetary policy, and geopolitical factors. The recent five-year window highlights gold's effectiveness during crisis periods, making it a strategically important portfolio component for investors concerned with wealth preservation amid economic uncertainty.
