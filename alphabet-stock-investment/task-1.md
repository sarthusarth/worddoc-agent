# Task 1: Investment Criteria for Tech Stocks

## Fundamental Analysis Metrics

When evaluating technology stocks, investors should focus on several core fundamentals that distinguish high-quality companies from speculative ones. Revenue growth rates are critical; technology companies with consistent growth in the 10-15% range or higher demonstrate underlying business strength. Equally important are profitability margins—leading tech firms often maintain operating margins above 40-45%, significantly higher than broader market averages of 18-20%. Free cash flow generation is essential, as it reveals whether earnings translate into actual cash available to shareholders after capital expenditure requirements. For technology companies, strong free cash flow margins (ideally above 20-25%) indicate efficient operations and sustainable business models. Additionally, investors should evaluate research and development spending as a percentage of revenue; tech companies must invest heavily in innovation, but this spending should yield measurable returns reflected in revenue growth and market share gains.

## Valuation Metrics Specific to Tech Stocks

Price-to-earnings (P/E) ratios remain foundational for tech stock valuation, though context matters significantly. Technology stocks typically trade at higher P/E multiples than broader market averages due to growth expectations; a tech company trading at 25x earnings may be reasonably valued if growing at 15% annually, while a mature tech firm at the same multiple might be overvalued. The Price-to-Fair-Value ratio, developed by research firms like Morningstar, offers a more comprehensive view by comparing current market price to intrinsic fair value based on discounted cash flows and competitive advantages. Tech stocks trading at a 5-10% discount to fair value represent more attractive entry points, while those trading at premiums above 10% warrant caution. Other relevant metrics include the Price-to-Sales ratio for earlier-stage tech companies with uncertain profitability, Price-to-Book ratios adjusted for intangible assets like patents and software, and Enterprise Value-to-EBITDA for comparing companies across different capital structures. Growth-adjusted valuation metrics—such as the PEG ratio (P/E divided by expected growth rate)—help distinguish between reasonably valued growth stocks and overvalued ones.

## Key Risk Factors in Technology Investing

Technology sector investments carry distinct risk factors that require careful assessment. Capital expenditure intensity represents a significant risk, particularly in cloud computing and artificial intelligence infrastructure; companies with rapidly escalating CapEx may face margin compression and reduced free cash flow generation despite strong revenue growth. Customer concentration risk is particularly acute in cloud and enterprise software, where losing a major customer—especially if representing 40% or more of revenue—can materially impact financial results and valuation. Technology stocks also face technological obsolescence risk; legacy business segments can experience accelerating revenue declines as new technologies emerge, forcing investors to rely increasingly on newer, unproven segments. Interest rate sensitivity poses another concern, as many tech companies are valued on the basis of distant future cash flows, making them particularly vulnerable to rising discount rates. Additionally, regulatory risks are escalating, including antitrust scrutiny of major technology platforms, data privacy regulations that increase compliance costs, and government restrictions on technology exports or partnerships. Competitive pressure in emerging technology areas like artificial intelligence could compress margins if multiple vendors commoditize what were once proprietary services.
